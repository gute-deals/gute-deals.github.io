Choosing a LEGO gift by age

Giving a LEGO set sounds easy. It gets easier if you settle a few questions first. This page helps you choose without recommending a particular set.

The tips on this page are general and have not been tested by us. What counts are always the details on the box and on the manufacturer's website.

Deutsche Fassung

The age rating on the box

Every set carries an age rating from the manufacturer, for example 6+, 12+ or 18+. It names the age the set is intended for. Among other things it takes into account how demanding the build is and whether the set contains small parts.

Take particular care with children under three, because small parts can be swallowed. The manufacturer offers separate ranges with larger bricks for this age group. Read the warnings on the box.

The age rating is a recommendation, not a test of ability. An experienced child can build a set meant for older children. If younger siblings live in the household, the question of small parts remains.

Building experience and patience

Besides age, the experience of the person receiving the gift matters. Someone who has never built from instructions is better off starting with a small set.

The piece count is printed on the box. As a rough guide: the more pieces, the more time and concentration the build needs. How long it really takes depends on practice and working style.

  • Small sets suit beginners and need little space.
  • Medium sets take more patience and a clear surface for the parts.
  • Very large sets are a project over several days and are aimed more at adults or very experienced builders.

Build, play or display?

Some sets are made for play, others for display. With display sets, for example from the Architecture or Botanicals ranges, the finished model is the point. Play sets are designed for playing and often include figures or moving parts.

Ask yourself: should the set stand on a shelf after the build, or be played with? This often influences the choice more than the theme.

Interests and theme

A set that matches a favourite theme is more likely to be used than one picked at random. The theme can be a film, a vehicle, an animal, a building or a hobby such as flowers.

If you are unsure, ask, for example parents, friends or a wish list. For collectors it matters which sets they already own, so that nothing arrives twice.

Space and timing

Plan where the building will happen and where the finished model will stand. Large models need space on the table and later on the shelf. Also check whether there is enough time to build around the birthday or holiday.

Checklist

  • ☐ Checked the age rating on the box
  • ☐ Considered small parts and warnings (younger children in the household?)
  • ☐ Chose a piece count that fits the building experience
  • ☐ Decided: build, play or display
  • ☐ Theme fits the interests
  • ☐ Planned space for the build and for the finished model
  • ☐ Made sure the person does not own the set yet

Frequently asked questions

May a child build a set meant for older children?

That is for you or the parents to decide. The age rating is a recommendation from the manufacturer. Pay particular attention to small parts if younger children live in the household.

How can I tell a display set?

The box and the manufacturer's product page say who a set is intended for. Sets for adults often carry the age rating 18+ and show a finished model for display.

What if a part is missing?

Check the bags and the instructions first. If something really is missing, the manufacturer usually offers a replacement parts service. Details are on the manufacturer's website.
