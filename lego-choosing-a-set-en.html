How to choose a LEGO set: pieces, theme and effort

The choice on the shelf or online is large. A few details on the box quickly show whether a set suits you or the person you are buying for.

The tips on this page are general and have not been tested by us. What counts are always the details on the box and on the manufacturer's website.

Deutsche Fassung

Start with the theme

Begin with what you like: a building, a vehicle, a film world, flowers, animals. A theme that excites you also helps through longer stages of the build.

The manufacturer sorts its sets into theme ranges. The product page shows which range a set belongs to.

Reading the piece count

The piece count tells you how many individual bricks and parts the set contains. It shows the scale of the build but is not a measure of difficulty: a set with many identical parts can be calmer to build than one with few but tricky parts.

  • Small sets suit beginners and need little space.
  • Large sets need time, calm and a clear surface for sorting the parts.

Age rating and difficulty

The manufacturer's age rating is a better guide to how demanding a set is than the piece count alone. More in the guide “Choosing a LEGO gift by age”.

Dimensions and space

The product page often lists the dimensions of the finished model. Measure the space on the shelf or sideboard before choosing a large display set.

Contents and special features

Read what belongs to the set: figures, stickers, printed parts or battery compartments. Parts with electronics need batteries, which are not included in every set.

Instructions and bags

In larger sets the bricks are often packed in numbered bags that belong to the stages of the instructions. Open the bags one after another. That keeps things tidy, and a missing part shows up early.

Checklist

  • ☐ The theme fits
  • ☐ Piece count and space fit the plan
  • ☐ Checked the age rating and warnings
  • ☐ Checked the dimensions of the finished model
  • ☐ Read the contents (figures, stickers, batteries)
  • ☐ Noted the set number (for instructions and spare parts)

Frequently asked questions

Where do I find the building instructions if I have lost them?

The manufacturer provides building instructions on its website. The set number, printed on the box and on the instructions, helps you find the right one.

Is a larger set automatically harder?

Not necessarily. The piece count shows the scale, not the difficulty. The manufacturer's age rating is the better guide.

Do I need glue or tools?

Not for building from the instructions: the bricks are clicked together. Gluing is neither intended nor needed.
